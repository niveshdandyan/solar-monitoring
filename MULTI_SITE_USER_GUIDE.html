1. Overview

The Multi-Site Comparison feature enables you to compare the performance of multiple solar installations side-by-side, regardless of their sizes. This is essential for:

  • Portfolio managers overseeing multiple solar sites
  • Identifying underperforming installations
  • Comparing performance across different locations and climates
  • Benchmarking new installations against existing ones

Why Normalized Metrics?

Directly comparing energy output from a 5 kWp residential system to a 500 kWp commercial installation is meaningless. Our system uses normalized metrics that account for system size, enabling "apples-to-apples" comparison:

Performance Ratio (PR)

Compares actual output to theoretical maximum, expressed as a percentage. A site with 85% PR produces 85% of what it theoretically could given the available sunlight.

Specific Yield (kWh/kWp)

Energy produced per unit of installed capacity. A site producing 4.5 kWh/kWp daily means each kWp of panels produces 4.5 kWh per day on average.

💡 Key Insight

A small 5 kWp rooftop with 90% PR is outperforming a large 500 kWp solar farm with 75% PR, even though the farm produces more total energy. The normalized metrics reveal this difference.

3. Getting Started

Accessing Multi-Site Comparison

1
Open the Application
Navigate to the Solar Panel Monitoring System in your browser.
2
Click the Multi-Site Button
In the sidebar under "Select Analysis Mode", click the 🏢 Multi-Site button.
3
Start Adding Sites
You'll see the Multi-Site Comparison interface with sections for adding and viewing sites.

4. Adding Sites

Step-by-Step Process

1
Upload CSV File
In the "Add Sites" section, click "Browse files" or drag-and-drop your CSV file.
2
Configure Site Name
Enter a descriptive name for the site (e.g., "Dubai Commercial Rooftop" or "Singapore Factory").
3
Set Nominal Power (kWp)
Enter the system's rated capacity in kilowatt-peak. This is critical for normalized calculations.
⚠️ Accuracy is Essential
An incorrect nominal power will skew all normalized metrics. Verify this value from system specifications.
4
Set Temperature Coefficient (Optional)
Default is -0.4%/°C (typical for crystalline silicon). Adjust if your panels have different specifications.
5
Click "Add This Site"
The system will process the data and add it to the comparison.
6
Repeat for Additional Sites
Add at least 2 sites to enable comparison. You can add up to 10 sites.

Managing Loaded Sites

The "Loaded Sites" panel shows all sites currently in the comparison:

  • View site details: Each site shows its name, nominal power, and date range
  • Remove individual sites: Click the ❌ button next to a site
  • Clear all sites: Use the "🗑️ Clear All Sites" button in the sidebar

5. Understanding the Metrics

1. Expected Power Calculation

The Expected Power (Pexp) is the theoretical power output of the system based on current environmental conditions. We use the standard temperature-corrected formula as defined in IEC 61724-1:

Pexp = PSTC × (G ÷ 1000) × [1 + γ × (Tcell − 25)]

Variables Explained:

Symbol Name Description Source
Pexp Expected Power Theoretical power output (Watts) Calculated
PSTC Nominal Power System rated capacity at Standard Test Conditions (Watts) User Input
G Irradiance Measured solar irradiance in plane of array (W/m²) CSV Data
1000 Reference Irradiance Standard irradiance at STC (W/m²) Constant
γ (gamma) Temp Coefficient Power temperature coefficient (e.g., -0.004 for -0.4%/°C) User Input
Tcell Cell Temperature Measured module/cell temperature (°C) CSV Data
25 Reference Temp Standard temperature at STC (°C) Constant

📝 Note on Temperature

If temperature data is missing, the temperature correction factor is assumed to be 1, meaning the formula simplifies to:

Pexp = PSTC × (G ÷ 1000)

2. Performance Ratio (PR)

The Performance Ratio is the primary metric for assessing system quality independent of location and size. It represents how efficiently the system converts available sunlight to energy.

PR (%) = (Measured Energy ÷ Expected Energy) × 100

Expanded Formula:

PR (%) = (∑ Emeasured ÷ ∑ Eexpected) × 100
Term Description How It's Calculated
Emeasured Measured Energy (kWh) Integration of measured power over time using trapezoidal rule
Eexpected Expected Energy (kWh) Integration of expected power (from formula above) over time
PR Range Rating Interpretation
≥ 80% 🟢 Excellent Well-maintained, high-quality system
70-80% 🟡 Good Normal performance, minor losses acceptable
60-70% 🟠 Fair Investigate potential issues
< 60% 🔴 Poor Significant issues - requires attention

3. Specific Yield (Yf)

Specific Yield measures energy production normalized by system size, allowing fair comparison between systems of different capacities.

Specific Yield = Total Energy (kWh) ÷ Nominal Power (kWp)
Term Description
Yf Specific Yield (kWh/kWp) - also called "Final Yield"
Edaily Total Daily Energy Output (kWh)
PSTC System Nominal Power (kWp)

Reference Ranges:

  • Daily: Typically 3-6 kWh/kWp depending on season and location
  • Annual: Typically 1,000-2,000 kWh/kWp depending on climate

📚 Industry Standard Reference

These formulas follow IEC 61724-1:2017 - Photovoltaic system performance — Part 1: Monitoring. This is the international standard for PV performance assessment.

Column Description

Side-by-side bar comparisons of key metrics:

  • Performance Ratio Comparison: Color-coded bars (green=excellent, yellow=good, orange=fair, red=poor)
  • Daily Specific Yield Comparison: Energy production per kWp by site

Best for: Quick visual ranking of sites

Time-series visualizations showing daily changes:

  • Daily PR Trend: PR values over time for all sites
  • Daily Specific Yield Trend: Energy production per kWp over time
  • Daily Normalized Power Trend: Maximum daily power per kWp

Best for: Identifying performance changes over time, seasonal patterns, or sudden drops

Average performance by hour of day:

  • Hourly Average Power per kWp: Normalized power curve showing when each site performs best
  • Power vs Irradiance Profiles: Side-by-side comparison of power and irradiance patterns

7. Interpreting Results

Common Date Range

The system automatically finds the overlapping date range across all sites. This ensures fair comparison by analyzing the same time period for all sites.

💡 Example

If Site A has data from Jan 1 - Dec 31 and Site B has data from Mar 15 - Nov 30, the comparison will use Mar 15 - Nov 30 (the overlapping period).

What Good Results Look Like

  • Similar PR across sites: Sites with similar equipment should have similar PR regardless of size
  • Higher yield in sunnier locations: Sites in Dubai should outproduce Tokyo in absolute terms
  • Consistent box plots: Narrow boxes indicate stable, well-maintained systems

Red Flags to Watch For

  • Sudden PR drops: May indicate equipment failure, soiling, or shading
  • High irradiance but low PR: System not converting available sunlight efficiently
  • Wide distribution in box plots: Inconsistent performance needs investigation
  • Morning/afternoon asymmetry in hourly profiles: May indicate partial shading

8. Troubleshooting & Common Issues

Issue Possible Cause Solution
PR shows 0% for all sites Power column not detected or all values are zero Ensure your CSV has a column named "Power", "Power (W)", or similar. Check that power values are non-zero during daytime.
"Error processing file" Invalid CSV format or encoding issues Save file as UTF-8 CSV. Ensure no special characters in column headers.
Date comparison error Timestamp format not recognized Use format: YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS or DD/MM/YYYY HH:MM:SS
No common date range found Sites have non-overlapping time periods Ensure all sites have data for at least some common dates
PR values seem too high (>100%) Incorrect nominal power or irradiance units Verify nominal power in kWp (not W). Check irradiance is in W/m² (not kW/m²)
Charts are empty Insufficient data after filtering Ensure sites have enough overlapping data points
Site not appearing after upload Processing failed silently Check the terminal for error messages. Verify file size is under 200MB.

Data Quality Checks

✅ Before uploading, verify:

  • File opens correctly in Excel/Numbers
  • Timestamps are in chronological order
  • No negative power values
  • Irradiance is 0 at night, positive during day
  • Temperature values are realistic (typically 15-60°C for cell temperature)

9. Best Practices

For Accurate Comparisons

  1. Use consistent data intervals: All sites should ideally have the same logging frequency (e.g., 15-minute intervals)
  2. Verify nominal power values: Double-check installed capacity from commissioning documents
  3. Compare similar time periods: Avoid comparing summer data from one site with winter data from another
  4. Consider local conditions: A site in a dusty environment may have lower PR than a clean environment
  5. Look at trends, not just averages: A site with declining PR needs attention even if the average is acceptable

For Portfolio Management

  • Compare sites monthly to catch issues early
  • Use the heatmap view to spot seasonal patterns
  • Focus on PR for comparing site quality
  • Focus on Specific Yield for revenue forecasting

Data Preparation Tips

  • Remove obviously erroneous data points before upload
  • Ensure complete data for the periods you want to compare
  • Use descriptive site names that clearly identify the installation

10. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How many sites can I compare at once?

The system supports up to 10 sites simultaneously. Beyond this, visualizations may become crowded.

Q: What's the minimum amount of data needed?

We recommend at least 7 days of data per site for meaningful comparison. Monthly comparisons require at least 30 days.

Q: Can I compare sites with different panel technologies?

Yes! Since we use normalized metrics, you can compare monocrystalline, polycrystalline, or thin-film installations fairly.

Q: Why does one site show higher yield but lower PR?

This typically happens when a site has more solar resource (higher irradiance) but converts it less efficiently. The site produces more absolute energy but underperforms relative to its potential.

Q: Can I export the comparison results?

Currently, you can screenshot the visualizations or copy the summary table. Full export functionality is planned for future releases.

Q: How is the "common date range" determined?

The system finds the latest start date and earliest end date across all sites, ensuring every site has data for the entire comparison period.

Q: What if my sites are in different time zones?

Ensure all timestamps are in local time for each site. The comparison focuses on solar hours (when irradiance > 0), which naturally aligns with local daytime.
